    Topic 1

    What technology transfer is

    Reading time: 2 min

    In one sentence: it is the path by which what is discovered in a lab reaches people, as a product, a service, a new company or a patent.

    Doing research and bringing it into real life are two different jobs. A brilliant lab result doesn’t turn into something you can use by itself: it must be protected, tested outside the lab, matched with someone who needs it and funded along the way.

    Universities and research centres usually have an office that helps with this (a transfer office, OTRI or OTC in Spain). Nivel 9 exists to accompany that path with clear information and tracking of available funding.

    Imaginary example

    Imagine a university group discovers a material that filters water better. That is a research result. To get it into a tap it must be protected, tested outside the lab, matched with a company that can make it, and funded all the way. That whole journey is technology transfer.

    To remember

    • Research and bringing it to market are two different jobs.
    • To reach people it must be protected, tested, matched with a user and funded.
    • Universities usually have an office that helps with this.
    Topic 2

    The nine levels: how far from working

    Reading time: 3 min

    In one sentence: a 1-to-9 scale showing how close a technology is to working outside the lab.

    It is called the technology readiness level (TRL) and the European Commission uses it in its research programmes. It answers a simple question: how far is this from working in the real world? Tap each level to see what it means. The name Nivel 9 comes from here: it is the goal.

    What level is your technology at?

    Level 9 of 9

    It works in the real world

    The real system is proven in its operational environment. For key technologies, that includes competitive manufacturing.

    Read how the levels are measured

    To remember

    • Level 1: the scientific principle is observed. Level 9: it works in the real world.
    • The higher the level, the closer to market.
    • Each funding call usually asks for a specific level.
    Topic 3

    The three paths from university to company

    Reading time: 3 min

    In one sentence: when research has commercial potential there are three usual routes: licence, spin-off or collaboration.

    • Licence

      A company uses your technology

      The institution keeps the patent and a company pays to use it. It fits when a company knows the market.

    • Spin-off

      A new company is born

      A company is created from the research centre. It fits when the technology is very promising and there is no clear industrial partner.

    • Collaboration

      A shared project

      An R&D&I project with a company, sharing risks and benefits. It fits when the company has a specific problem.

    Summary of a popular-science guide by the Coral community. Read the full article (in Spanish).

    To remember

    • No path is better than another: it depends on the technology and the team.
    • A patent protects the invention; a licence lets others use it in exchange for payment.
    Topic 4

    Who helps and how it is funded

    Reading time: 3 min

    In one sentence: besides your university’s office, there is public funding, called calls, with deadlines.

    Bringing a technology to market takes time and money. That is why calls exist: public offers of funding with a deadline, requirements and a budget.

    • National: issued, among others, by the Spanish State Research Agency (for example, projects and contracts for researchers).
    • European: Horizon Europe. The European Research Council (ERC) supports researchers; the European Innovation Council (EIC) supports technologies close to market and companies.

    See the deadline calendar and what each grant is

    To remember

    • A call is a public offer of funding with a deadline.
    • Always confirm dates and requirements in the official call.

    Topic 6

    Glossary: the key words

    Reading time: 2 min

    In one sentence: eight terms you will see again and again.

    Transfer office (OTRI / OTC)
    Office at a university or centre that helps protect research results and bring them to market.
    Patent
    A right that protects an invention for a set period.
    Licence
    Permission for another organisation to use a technology in return for payment.
    Spin-off
    A new company created from a centre’s research results.
    Call
    A public offer of funding with a deadline, requirements and a budget.
    R&D&I
    Research, development and innovation.
    Consortium
    A group of organisations that join to submit and carry out a project together.
    Readiness level (TRL)
    A 1-to-9 scale of how close a technology is to working in the real world.

    To remember

    • If a term isn’t here, ask us by email and we’ll add it.
